## Founding and Historical Development

**Founding Origins**

Cushman & Wakefield was incorporated on October 31, 1917, in New York City by John Clydesdale ("Clyde") Cushman and his brother-in-law Bernard Wakefield[1][2]. The founders established the company with a passion for property management and a commitment to applying scientific methodology to the discipline. Cushman articulated the firm's foundational principles in a 1922 book titled *Management: How Modern Business Buildings Are Operated*[2].

**Early Expansion**

The company began as a property management and leasing agent for prominent New York City buildings during the 1920s[1][2]. A pivotal figure in the firm's growth was Leone Peters, who joined during the Great Depression and rose through the ranks over a 59-year career to eventually become chairman[1]. His brothers Anthony and Vincent also contributed to the company's development. Additional offices opened in Atlanta, Chicago, San Francisco, and Puerto Rico during this expansion phase[1].

**Mid-to-Late 20th Century Growth**

In 1926, Cushman & Wakefield opened a rental office in the General Motors building and won the "Most Ingenious Deal of Year" award for facilitating the sale of land for the United Nations project[2][3]. The company expanded into the downtown financial district in 1932 when it established a branch office at 30 Broad Street in the Continental Bank Building[2].

The firm experienced significant ownership transitions: RCA Corporation acquired Cushman & Wakefield in 1969, then sold its stake to The Rockefeller Group in 1976[3]. As a Rockefeller Group company, Cushman & Wakefield expanded considerably during the 1980s, growing to more than 40 offices throughout the United States and adding financial services operations in 1983[1]. The company made its first international move in early 1984 through a joint venture with a Hong Kong real estate firm[1].

**Modern Era**

The firm's predecessor operations trace back further: DTZ, which merged with Cushman & Wakefield, has roots extending to 1784 with the founding of Chesshire Gibson in Birmingham, UK, and includes Healey & Baker (founded 1820) and Debenham and Tewson (founded 1853)[3][8]. In 1987, Debenham, Tewson & Chinnocks (DTZ) was listed on the London Stock Exchange[3]. Mitsubishi Estate Co. Ltd became The Rockefeller Group's majority shareholder in 1989[3]. By 1994, Cushman & Wakefield had established worldwide partnerships with major real estate firms across the Americas, Europe, and Asia[3].
